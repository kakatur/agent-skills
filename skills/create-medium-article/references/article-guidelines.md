# Medium Article Guidelines

## Editorial position

Write a standalone article with one clear reader promise. Use project sources
as evidence and raw material, but do not reproduce a lesson, video, README,
deck, or implementation file section by section.

## Voice

- Open with a concrete situation, question, decision, or failure the reader
  can picture.
- Prefer familiar words and direct sentences over formal abstraction.
- Explain necessary domain terms in plain language without weakening them.
- Keep paragraphs short enough for comfortable screen reading.
- Use an informed, conversational voice rather than textbook exposition or
  promotional copy.

## Shape

Choose only the elements the subject needs:

1. Specific title
2. Subtitle or deck
3. Hero image note with filename, alt text, and caption
4. Opening problem or decision
5. Core mental model or argument
6. Practical examples, patterns, trade-offs, or pitfalls
7. Focused code or evidence when useful
8. Checklist or decision guide when it adds non-duplicative value
9. Direct source, implementation, or next-step links
10. Closing consequence, decision, or takeaway

This is a menu, not a quota. A shorter article with one strong path is better
than an exhaustive article assembled from every available source.

## Titles

Create tension or curiosity through a real consequence, constraint, mistake,
or decision. Keep the promise honest. Avoid vague introductions, unsupported
superlatives, and listicle framing that the article does not deliver.

## Repetition

Give each important idea one clear home. Remove:

- a second analogy after the first one works
- a paragraph that restates the previous paragraph more abstractly
- a list that repeats surrounding prose
- extra caveats after the practical boundary is already clear
- a conclusion that recites every section

Every paragraph should add an idea, example, decision rule, consequence, or
necessary qualification.

## Images

Prefer one clean, wide editorial image that communicates the subject without
tiny labels or dense embedded text. Avoid generic AI imagery and unlicensed
logos or assets. Include useful alt text and a short caption.

## Links and attribution

Link directly to the most relevant public page, implementation folder, video,
or source instead of only a homepage. Keep promotional links brief and useful.
Preserve attribution and distinguish sourced facts from inference or advice.
