# Written Prose

Use this mode for articles, essays, documentation, newsletters, and other text
designed to be read on a page.

## Preserve

- Markdown structure, frontmatter, links, citations, code blocks, tables, and
  technical identifiers.
- The author's point of view and degree of certainty.
- Concrete examples that provide evidence or teach a distinct idea.
- Project terminology and any protected wording in the content profile.

## Improve

- Open with a concrete situation, question, observation, or failure the reader
  can recognize.
- Prefer familiar words and direct sentences when meaning stays intact.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one idea.
- Use contractions and direct address only when they fit the established
  voice.
- Make transitions arise from the preceding idea rather than announcing the
  next section mechanically.
- Give each important idea one clear home.

## Rewrite or remove

- Textbook or trend openings that delay the actual subject.
- Inflated importance claims and promotional adjectives unsupported by
  evidence.
- Empty signposts such as "let's dive in" or "without further ado."
- Vague authority such as "experts say" without a source.
- Rule-of-three filler, synonym cycling, false ranges, and manufactured
  punchlines.
- Lists that repeat surrounding prose and paragraphs that restate the previous
  paragraph abstractly.
- Conclusions that summarize every section without adding a decision, next
  step, consequence, or useful closing insight.

Do not enforce a phrase blacklist mechanically. Keep a phrase when it is the
clearest accurate choice in context.

## Final check

Read once for natural flow and once for semantic drift. Confirm that editing
did not alter code, links, attribution, qualifications, or factual scope.
